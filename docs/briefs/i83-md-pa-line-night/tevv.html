Definitions: what the benchmark table's rows, columns and names mean

Benchmark row (BENCH-n):
one test of hazbrief against a published result. BENCH-0 is the requirement the others serve: as a minimum, hazbrief does as well as each accepted tool; as its objective, better (its state is note 5).
Test:
the benchmark row's id and a plain name for it.
Run:
the model run being compared (the names are below); empty where no run is held.
Compared with:
the trial's measurements, or a tool's published run of the same trial (why these tools: note 3).
Arcs (n):
how many sampling arcs, at fixed distances from the release, are compared; each arc gives its maximum concentration.
Ratio:
against the measurements, the geometric mean of measured over predicted concentration (MG); against a tool, the run's MG divided by the tool's. 1 means no difference; above 1 means the run's concentrations are lower.
95 percent limits:
the range the ratio could plausibly be in, at the 95 percent confidence level (the method: note 2).
Within a factor of two (k of n):
how many arcs' predicted maximum is within a factor of two of the measured one; a count, not a test.
Statistically different?:
Yes when the 95 percent limits leave out 1, with below or above for the run's concentrations; No when they include 1; Not yet tested where no run is held.
Note:
the numbers of the notes below the table that bear on the row.
SCICHEM:
hazbrief's run of SCICHEM, the puff model that draws a dense release's cloud on the brief.
SLAB RH 20, SLAB RH 80:
hazbrief's run of SLAB, the dense-gas model (Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, 1990), at 20 or 80 percent relative humidity.
HPAC complex, HPAC simple:
HPAC's two published runs of the trial: with a complex source (the 17-model comparison) and a simple jet-only source (Simpson et al. 2020).
ALOHA:
ALOHA's (Areal Locations of Hazardous Atmospheres) published run of the trial in the 17-model comparison.
