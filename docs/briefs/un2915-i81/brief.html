ERG 2024 Guide 163: Radioactive Materials (Low to High Level Radiation)

Potential hazards

HEALTH

  • Radiation presents minimal risk to transport workers, emergency response personnel and the public during transportation accidents. Packaging durability increases as potential hazard of radioactive content increases. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Undamaged packages are safe. Contents of damaged packages may cause higher external radiation exposure, or both external and internal radiation exposure if contents are released. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Type A packages (cartons, boxes, drums, articles, etc.) identified as Type A by marking on packages or by shipping papers contain non-life-endangering amounts. Partial releases might be expected if Type A packages are damaged in moderately severe accidents. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Type B packages, and the rarely occurring Type C packages (large and small, usually metal), contain the most hazardous amounts. They can be identified by package markings or by shipping papers. Life-threatening conditions may exist only if contents are released or package shielding fails. Because of design, evaluation and testing of packages, these conditions would be expected only for accidents of utmost severity. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • The rarely occurring "Special Arrangement" shipments may be of Type A, Type B or Type C packages. Package type will be marked on packages, and shipment details will be on shipping papers. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Radioactive White-I labels indicate radiation levels outside single, isolated, undamaged packages are very low (less than 0.005 mSv/h (0.5 mrem/h)). [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Radioactive Yellow-II and Yellow-III labeled packages have higher radiation levels. The transport index (TI) on the label identifies the maximum radiation level in mrem/h one meter from a single, isolated, undamaged package. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Some radioactive materials cannot be detected by commonly available instruments. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Water from cargo fire control may cause pollution. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

FIRE OR EXPLOSION

  • Some of these materials may burn, but most do not ignite readily. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Radioactivity does not change flammability or other properties of materials. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Type B packages are designed and evaluated to withstand total engulfment in flames at temperatures of 800°C (1475°F) for a period of 30 minutes. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

Public safety

  • CALL 911. Then call emergency response telephone number on shipping paper. If shipping paper not available or no answer, refer to appropriate telephone number listed on the inside back cover. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Priorities for rescue, life-saving, first aid, fire control and other hazards are higher than the priority for measuring radiation levels. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Radiation Authority must be notified of accident conditions. Radiation Authority is usually responsible for decisions about radiological consequences and closure of emergencies. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Stay upwind, uphill and/or upstream. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]
  • Detain or isolate uninjured persons or equipment suspected to be contaminated; delay decontamination and cleanup until instructions are received from Radiation Authority. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

PROTECTIVE CLOTHING

  • Positive pressure self-contained breathing apparatus (SCBA) and structural firefighters’ protective clothing will provide adequate protection against internal radiation exposure, but not external radiation exposure. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

EVACUATION

Immediate precautionary measure

  • Isolate spill or leak area for at least 25 meters (75 feet) in all directions. [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

Large Spill

  • Consider initial downwind evacuation for at least 100 meters (330 feet). [erg2024-en-pdf#p258]

Emergency response

FIRE

  • Presence of radioactive material will not influence the fire control processes and should not influence selection of techniques. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • If it can be done safely, move undamaged containers away from the area around the fire. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Do not move damaged packages; move undamaged packages out of fire zone. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]

Small Fire

  • Dry chemical, CO2, water spray or regular foam. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]

Large Fire

  • Water spray, fog (flooding amounts). [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Dike runoff from fire control for later disposal. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]

SPILL OR LEAK

  • Do not touch damaged packages or spilled material. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Damp surfaces on undamaged or slightly damaged packages are seldom an indication of packaging failure. Most packaging for liquid content have inner containers and/or inner absorbent materials. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Cover liquid spill with sand, earth or other non-combustible absorbent material. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]

FIRST AID

Refer to the “General First Aid” section.

Specific First Aid:

  • Medical problems take priority over radiological concerns. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Use first aid treatment according to the nature of the injury. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Do not delay care and transport of a seriously injured person. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
  • Injured persons contaminated by contact with released material are not a serious hazard to health care personnel, equipment or facilities. [erg2024-en-pdf#p259]
